Before you start — check this box list

  • ✓A fresh wallet just for deploying. Never your main or vault wallet.
  • ✓1–2 POL on Polygon mainnet in that wallet, for gas. (Gas = the small fee the network charges for every transaction.)
  • ✓Node.js installed on your computer (version 18 or newer).
  • ✓The repo cloned and the compiled file nft/VAKED.bin present. It is already compiled — you do not compile anything.
  • ✓The private key of your deploy wallet handy. (A private key is the secret password for a wallet. Anyone who sees it can spend everything in it — keep it safe.)
1

Get a wallet and put gas money in it

safe to redo

Make a fresh wallet that is only used for this deployment — a "deployer" wallet. Then send it 1–2 POL, Polygon's coin, on Polygon mainnet. The network charges a small fee called gas for every transaction, and that fee is paid in POL. A deploy costs a fraction of a POL, so 1–2 POL is plenty of room.

Why a fresh wallet? If anything goes wrong, the damage is limited to a small amount of test money. Your real funds stay untouched.

2

Dry run first — spend nothing

safe to redo

Run the deploy script in dry-run mode. It signs the transaction and prints exactly what it would do, but it sends nothing and costs nothing. You will see your wallet address, the predicted contract address (the address the token will live at once it exists — it is calculated from your wallet and your transaction count, so it is known before the deploy), and the gas estimate.

$ PRIVATE_KEY=0x... node nft/deploy.js --vaked

Read the output. The predicted address should look like a normal address (0x...) and should match what you expect.

Why bother? This is the free rehearsal. If something is wrong, you find out now, not after real money moves.

3

Broadcast for real

safe to redo

Same command, one extra flag: --broadcast. Now the script actually sends the transaction to the network and pays gas with your POL. It prints a transaction hash — a long string that is the receipt for your transaction, like a tracking number. Anyone can look it up on polygonscan.com.

$ PRIVATE_KEY=0x... node nft/deploy.js --vaked --broadcast

Wait for the transaction to be confirmed, then check polygonscan: your contract should exist at the predicted address.

Why bother? If the deploy fails (for example, you forgot gas), nothing is lost — your wallet keeps its remaining POL and you can simply try again.

4

Verify the source code

safe to redo

Right now, the network only knows the compiled machine code of your contract. Verification publishes the human-readable source code next to it on Sourcify or Polygonscan. Then anyone can compare the source to the real bytecode and check the fair-launch promises for themselves:

a 21,000,000 token cap, no premine (nobody — including the deployer — holds tokens before the public starts mining), and a proof-of-work mint (tokens are only created by solving a puzzle, the same pattern as early Bitcoin).

Why bother? This is what makes VAKED "verifiable, not promised". An unverified contract asks you to trust; a verified one lets you check.

5

Test a few mints

live network — be careful

Before you hand over control, mine a few tokens on the live network to confirm mining works. The repo has a miner: miner/mine.js. Point it at your deployed contract and let it solve one or two proof-of-work puzzles. Each mint costs only gas — no fee, no tax.

$ node miner/mine.js --rpc <rpc-url> --contract <deployed-address> --key <privkey>

Check that mints confirm and that the difficulty adjustment behaves — after a while, the puzzle difficulty should move toward the target of roughly one mint per 60 blocks. This is the moment to notice any surprises, while you still own the contract and could react.

Why bother? Once ownership is gone, you cannot fix anything. This is your last chance to see the machine run.

6

Renounce ownership — last, and forever

final — cannot be undone
You cannot undo this

Calling renounceOwnership() sets the owner to address(0) — a black hole address nobody controls. After this, nobody, not even you, can change the contract, its settings, or its ownership. There is no "oops" button.

Do this only after steps 1–5 are fully confirmed: deployed, verified, and tested. And do not bundle it into the deploy transaction — renounce as a separate, deliberate step, days after you are calm about everything else.

Why bother? Renouncing is what makes the fair launch real: after this, the token is not "we promise it's fair" — it is permanently, mechanically fair. This step converts trust into math.

Things that can go wrong

No gas in the wallet

If the wallet runs out of POL, the transaction fails and nothing is deployed. Fix: top up with POL and try again. No harm done.

Wrong or lost private key

If the key is wrong, the transaction is invalid. If the key is lost or leaked, the wallet (and anything in it) is gone or stolen. Fix: keep the key offline and never paste it anywhere public.

Renouncing too early

If you renounce before testing, a bug in the minting or difficulty logic becomes permanent. Fix: there is no fix. That is why step 6 comes last and alone.
